IInspiral in the disk

Gas torques in the AGN disk harden the stellar-mass binary at radius RBH ~ 10²–10⁴ rg from the SMBH until gravitational-wave emission takes over and drives it to merger. The binary carries a Hill sphere of bound disk gas, RHill = RBH(q/3)1/3.

IIMerger & recoil

The merger itself emits no light — only gravitational waves. Anisotropic GW emission kicks the remnant at vk ~ 10²–10³ km s−1. Gas within Rbound ≈ 0.34 RHill(q/10⁻⁶)2/3(RBH/10³)−1(vk/200)−2 stays bound and is dragged along.

IIIRam-pressure stripping

Crossing the disk, the bound gas is shock-heated and stripped over tram ≈ 20 (MBH/100)(vk/200)−3 days, leaving an off-center hot spot at T ≈ 10⁵ (vk/100 km s−1) K. This delayed UV/optical transient — the Dark Flare — is the only electromagnetic counterpart of the merger.

IVDark Flare & decay

The hot spot is modeled as a blackbody of radius Rbound, redshifted, extincted (FM07, AV=1) and convolved with LSST g/r/i throughputs. The light curve rises as sin²(πt/2tram) and decays as exp(−t/τ), with τ = RHillRg/vk — the Graham et al. (2020) ZTF assumptions.
